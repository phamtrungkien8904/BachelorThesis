\chapter*{Abstract}
\phantomsection\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{\protect Abstract}
\thispagestyle{plain}

Organic semiconductors are promising materials for lightweight, flexible, and
large-area electronics. In organic field-effect transistors (OFETs), however,
the measured current is not determined by the semiconductor channel alone.
Contacts, device geometry, the gate dielectric, localized states, leakage paths,
and bias history can all influence transfer and output characteristics. Mobility
values extracted from conventional two-terminal data are therefore
model-dependent and may describe an apparent device response rather than the
transport properties of the accumulated semiconductor sheet.

This thesis asks whether current-driven gated van der Pauw measurements can
characterize organic thin-film transistors in a way that is less dominated by
contact effects. Bottom-gate C8-BTBT OFETs were fabricated and investigated by
combining device-quality checks, conventional current--voltage measurements,
four-terminal sheet-conductance analysis, background-resistance extraction, and
transient circuit tests. The aim was to connect transistor operation,
contact-related limitations, and dynamic relaxation within one consistent
experimental platform.

The fabricated devices show clear $p$-type field-effect behaviour with low gate
leakage and ordered transfer and output characteristics. At the same time, their
gradual turn-on and finite background resistance indicate that two-terminal
analysis remains sensitive to contact and series contributions. In contrast, the
gated van der Pauw conductance measured with imposed currents of
\SI{-100}{\nano\ampere}, \SI{-200}{\nano\ampere}, and
\SI{-300}{\nano\ampere} collapses onto a common linear dependence on effective
gate bias. The extracted mobility is approximately
\SI{3.1}{\centi\metre\squared\per\volt\per\second}, with a threshold voltage near
\SI{-10.2}{\volt}. Analysis of the full current-injection path gives a residual
resistance between \SI{5.0}{\mega\ohm} and \SI{5.8}{\mega\ohm}, consistent with
the background resistance obtained from the two-terminal linear-regime analysis.

Transient and amplifier measurements show that the response is not purely
static. Slow relaxation, parasitic capacitances, and large circuit resistances
affect the time-dependent behaviour and must be considered when interpreting
nominally static transistor curves or using OFETs in circuits. Overall, the
results show that the gated van der Pauw method can provide a more robust
description of the accumulated semiconductor sheet, provided that current
dependence, residual series resistance, leakage, and transient effects are
checked explicitly.
