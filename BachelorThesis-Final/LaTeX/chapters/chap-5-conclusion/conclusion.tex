\chapter{Conclusion and Summary}

This thesis investigated the contact-independent electrical characterization
of gate-induced transport in C8-BTBT organic thin films using the
current-driven gated van der Pauw method. The central objective was to determine
whether the sheet conductance and long-range carrier mobility of the accumulated
semiconductor film could be extracted without directly including voltage drops
at the current-injecting contacts.

The fabricated bottom-gate structures showed reproducible $p$-type
field-effect operation, low gate leakage, and ordered transfer and output
characteristics.
These measurements established that the devices were suitable for gated
transport measurements. At the same time, the gradual turn-on and the finite
background contribution in the two-terminal resistance showed that conventional
two-terminal field-effect characteristics contain contact and series
contributions. The
two-terminal measurements therefore served primarily as a contact-sensitive
reference and as a test of device operation.

The central gated van der Pauw measurements were performed with imposed
currents of $-100\,\mathrm{nA}$, $-200\,\mathrm{nA}$, and
$-300\,\mathrm{nA}$. When plotted against the effective gate voltage corrected
by the mean potential of the probed region, the resulting sheet-conductance
curves approximately collapsed onto a common linear dependence. The agreement
between the current series supports operation within a linear-response regime
and shows that the extracted transport parameters are not determined by the
selected measurement current. From the conductance slope, a
contact-independent long-range thin-film mobility of approximately
$3.1\,\mathrm{cm^2\,V^{-1}\,s^{-1}}$ was obtained, together with a threshold
voltage near $-10.2\,\mathrm{V}$.

The complete current-injection path remained contact-sensitive. Its analysis
gave a residual resistance between approximately $5.0\,\mathrm{M\Omega}$ and
$5.8\,\mathrm{M\Omega}$, in agreement with the background contribution inferred
from the conventional linear-regime measurements. This comparison illustrates
the central distinction between the longitudinal voltage required to inject the
current and the transverse voltage used to determine the sheet conductance.

The complementary transient and amplifier measurements showed that the
electrical response is not purely static. Slow relaxation, parasitic capacitances, and
large external resistances affect the temporal response and can influence the
interpretation of measurements when insufficient settling times are used.
These effects do not invalidate the gated van der Pauw extraction, but they
demonstrate that measurement history and circuit time constants must be
controlled.

The scientific questions can therefore be answered as follows. The fabricated
structures provide suitable field-effect modulation and sufficiently low
leakage for gated van der Pauw measurements. Within the investigated
accumulation regime, the four-terminal sheet conductance gives a reproducible
and approximately current-independent mobility extraction. Compared with
conventional two-terminal characterization, the method is substantially less
sensitive to voltage drops at the injecting contacts. Nevertheless,
\emph{contact-independent} does not mean that the contacts, film morphology,
traps, leakage, or injection conditions are irrelevant. The extracted value
represents the long-range mobility of the accumulated C8-BTBT thin-film sheet,
including transport barriers within the film, rather than the intrinsic
intra-grain mobility of the molecular semiconductor.

Overall, the results demonstrate that the current-driven gated van der Pauw
method is an effective approach for electrically characterizing organic thin
films when contact-related voltage losses make conventional two-terminal
parameter extraction ambiguous. The result is a characterization of the
organic thin film within its complete gated device environment, rather than a
claim of microscopic intragrain transport.
