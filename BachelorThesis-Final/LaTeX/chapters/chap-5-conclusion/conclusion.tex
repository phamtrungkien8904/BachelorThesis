\chapter{Conclusion and Summary}
\thispagestyle{plain}

This thesis investigated whether the transport properties of organic thin-film
transistors can be measured in a way that describes the accumulated
semiconductor sheet, rather than being dominated by contacts and
measurement-history effects. The motivation was that conventional two-terminal
OFET measurements do not probe only the semiconductor channel. Contact voltage
drops, gate-dependent injection, localized states, leakage, and transient
charging can all change the apparent slope of transfer curves and therefore
influence extracted mobility and threshold-voltage values
\cite{Natali2012ChargeInjection,Bittle2016MobilityOverestimation}. The gated van
der Pauw method was used as the central analysis tool because it determines the
sheet conductance from voltage probes that do not carry the imposed current
\cite{Rolin2017GVDP,Lee2024GVDPIGZO}.

The work combined theoretical analysis, setup characterization, device
fabrication, and electrical measurements on bottom-gate C8-BTBT OFETs. The
investigated stack consisted of a Cr gate electrode, a PaN/PS gate dielectric, a
C8-BTBT molecular semiconductor layer, and MoO$_x$/Au injection contacts. The
clover-leaf geometry allowed conventional source--drain measurements and gated
van der Pauw measurements to be performed on the same device type. This made it
possible to compare contact-sensitive two-terminal data with a four-terminal
sheet-conductance extraction and with the resistance of the full current path.

The first scientific question concerned stable field-effect operation. The
fabricated devices fulfilled the basic requirements for further analysis: the
gate leakage remained small compared with the source--drain current, negative
gate bias accumulated holes at the semiconductor--dielectric interface, and the
transfer and output characteristics showed clear $p$-type transistor behaviour.
At the same time, the gradual turn-on of the transfer curves and the finite
background resistance showed that the two-terminal curves contain nonideal
contributions. Conventional measurements therefore verified transistor action,
but were not sufficient on their own for a reliable mobility interpretation.

The second and third questions concerned contact influence, four-terminal sheet
transport, and dynamic response. The gated van der Pauw measurements provided the
main quantitative result. Sheet conductance curves measured at
$I_{34}=\SI{-100}{\nano\ampere}$, $\SI{-200}{\nano\ampere}$, and
$\SI{-300}{\nano\ampere}$ collapsed onto a common approximately linear
dependence on effective gate voltage. The extracted mobilities were
$(3.11 \pm 0.02)~\si{cm^2/Vs}$, $(3.11 \pm 0.02)~\si{cm^2/Vs}$, and
$(3.10 \pm 0.02)~\si{cm^2/Vs}$, with threshold voltages near
\SI{-10.2}{\volt} and $R^2>0.998$ for all fits. Within the measured current
range and contact configuration, the extracted mobility is therefore
approximately \SI{3.1}{\centi\metre\squared\per\volt\per\second} and is not an
artefact of the chosen drive current.

The analysis of the full current-injection path showed that the four-terminal
result should still be interpreted together with series contributions outside
the probed sheet resistance. The total resistance decreased with increasing gate
overdrive and was described by
$R_\mathrm{tot}=R_\infty+A/(V_\mathrm{eff}-V_\mathrm{T})$. Separate fits gave
$R_\infty=(5.00\pm0.23)~\si{\mega\ohm}$,
$(5.28\pm0.22)~\si{\mega\ohm}$, and
$(5.77\pm0.30)~\si{\mega\ohm}$ for
$I_{34}=\SI{-100}{\nano\ampere}$, $\SI{-200}{\nano\ampere}$, and
$\SI{-300}{\nano\ampere}$, respectively. These values are consistent with the
approximately \SI{5.1}{\mega\ohm} background resistance found from the
two-terminal linear-regime analysis. The agreement supports the interpretation
that both analyses identify a similar gate-independent series contribution,
although the individual contact resistance was not separated directly.

The transient and amplifier measurements showed that the devices cannot be
described as purely static circuit elements. The DLTS-type experiment exhibited
a fast capacitive response followed by slower current relaxation, consistent with
localized states that exchange carriers with the channel over a distribution of
time scales~\cite{Lang1974DLTS,Hirwa2015TrapStates,Bobbert2012OperationalStability}.
The simple amplifier test demonstrated inverted voltage amplification, but the
square-wave response showed finite rise and fall times caused by large circuit
resistances, parasitic capacitances, and trap-related relaxation. These results
show that bias history and circuit time constants must be considered when
interpreting nominally static transfer curves or using these OFETs in circuits.

Overall, the main question can be answered positively within the limits of the
measured device, bias range, and current range. The gated van der Pauw method
provided a reproducible sheet conductance and a current-independent mobility for
the accumulated C8-BTBT film, while the two-terminal and full-current-path
analyses revealed residual series contributions. The method therefore gives a
more robust description of the semiconductor sheet than conventional
two-terminal analysis alone, but it does not remove the need to check current
dependence, leakage, residual resistance, and transient relaxation explicitly.

The three working hypotheses are supported with these qualifications. First, the
gated van der Pauw sheet conductance increased approximately linearly with
effective gate bias, and mobilities extracted at three imposed currents agreed
within their uncertainties. Second, the two-terminal characteristics were more
sensitive to nonideal contact and series contributions, while the gated van der
Pauw current-path analysis gave a consistent residual resistance. Third,
DLTS-type and amplifier measurements confirmed qualitatively that slow relaxation
and external circuit elements influence the device response, although a complete
quantitative trap-state distribution was not extracted.

Several limitations remain. The number of devices and current levels was
limited, so a broader statistical study is needed to separate device-to-device
variation from systematic material behaviour. The numerical simulation used a
simplified two-dimensional model and did not include microscopic injection
physics, trap energetics, or the full gate-stack electrostatics. Future work
should therefore combine gated van der Pauw measurements with temperature,
contact-metal, sweep-rate, frequency-response, and device-statistics studies,
and with more complete drift-diffusion--Poisson simulations that include contact
injection and trap-state distributions.
