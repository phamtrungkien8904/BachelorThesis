\section{Scientific Questions}
\label{sec:scientific_questions}

The scientific questions of this thesis are highlighted as follows:

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Main question:} Can the transport properties of organic thin-film
transistors be measured in a way that describes the accumulated semiconductor
sheet, rather than being dominated by contact effects and measurement-history
effects?

To answer this, the thesis focuses on three subquestions:
\begin{description}
    \item[\textbf{Q1 -- Field-effect operation:}] Do the fabricated devices and the
    measurement setup show stable field-effect operation with sufficiently low
    gate leakage?
    \item[\textbf{Q2 -- Contact influence:}] How strongly do contact resistance
    and charge injection affect the interpretation of conventional transfer and
    output characteristics, including threshold-voltage estimates?
    \item[\textbf{Q3 -- Four-terminal and dynamic response:}] Does the gated van
    der Pauw method give a more contact-independent sheet conductance and
    mobility, does the full current path reveal a comparable background
    resistance, and do trap-related transients or circuit time constants have to
    be considered?
\end{description}
\end{quote}

Demonstrating field-effect behaviour alone is not enough to answer these
questions. The following paragraphs explain how the experimental platform, the
contact geometry, and transient effects are tested.

The first part concerns the experimental platform. Before the
gated van der Pauw method can be applied to molecular semiconductor devices, the
prototype setup has to be checked for stable current driving and reliable
voltage detection. On the fabrication side, the devices have to be produced in a
layer-by-layer process that combines physical vapor deposition of the molecular
semiconductor and metal contacts, chemical vapor deposition of the parylene-N
insulator, and spin coating of the planarization layer. The completed structures
must then show low gate leakage, reproducible field-effect modulation, and
ordered transfer and output characteristics. Without these basic conditions,
neither conventional transistor analysis nor gated van der Pauw analysis would
give meaningful transport parameters.

The second part concerns the role of the contacts. In a two-terminal
measurement, the same electrodes inject current and define the measured voltage.
Injection barriers and contact resistance can therefore change the slope and
shape of transfer and output curves, complicating threshold-voltage estimates
and any comparison with ideal FET relations.
The gated van der Pauw measurement tests whether a sheet conductance obtained
from non-injecting voltage probes gives a more robust result. If measurements at
different imposed currents agree, this is an important indication that the
extracted mobility reflects the accumulated film rather than a contact-limited
current path. The remaining resistance measured between the current-injecting
contacts is analysed separately, so that the gate-dependent channel response can
be compared with any gate-independent background contribution.

The third part concerns dynamic effects. Organic semiconductors often contain
localized states that can trap and release carriers over long time scales. Such
processes can influence threshold-voltage extraction, hysteresis, and circuit
response even when a static transfer curve appears smooth
\cite{Hirwa2015TrapStates,Bobbert2012OperationalStability}. DLTS-type transient
measurements and a simple amplifier test are therefore used to examine whether
slow relaxation and external circuit elements affect the device response.

The work is guided by three working hypotheses. First, in a suitable
accumulation regime, the sheet conductance measured by the gated van der Pauw
method should increase approximately linearly with effective gate bias, and the
mobility extracted from this slope should be similar for different sufficiently
small measurement currents. Second, conventional two-terminal analysis should be
more sensitive to contact and series effects than the four-terminal gated van der
Pauw analysis. Evidence for this would include a finite background resistance,
nonideal transfer or output curves, or a discrepancy between contact-sensitive
and contact-independent interpretations; consistency between the background
resistance obtained from two-terminal and gated van der Pauw current-path
analysis would strengthen this interpretation. Third, transient and amplifier
measurements should show whether slow trap-related relaxation and external
circuit time constants are relevant for the fabricated devices and whether they
have to be considered when interpreting nominally static measurements.

The following chapters test these hypotheses through controlled comparisons.
The key checks are the stability of the measurement setup, the integrity of the
fabricated device stack, the magnitude of the gate leakage, the shape of the
transfer and output curves, the linearity of the gated van der Pauw conductance,
the current independence of the extracted mobility, the residual resistance of
the current-injection path, and the presence or absence of slow transient
relaxation. In this way, the thesis connects setup characterization,
fabrication, measurement geometry, circuit response, and data analysis to the
question of how reliably transport parameters can be obtained from organic
thin-film transistors.
