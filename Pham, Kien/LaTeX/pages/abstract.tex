Organic semiconductor thin films are promising active materials for lightweight,
flexible, and large-area electronics. Their quantitative electrical
characterization is nevertheless challenging because measurements performed on
complete devices can be influenced by metal-semiconductor contacts, device
geometry, the gate dielectric, localized states, leakage paths, and bias history.
In conventional two-terminal organic field-effect transistor measurements, the
extracted mobility may therefore represent an apparent device response rather
than the long-range transport properties of the gate-induced semiconductor
sheet.

This thesis investigates the contact-independent electrical characterization
of gated C8-BTBT thin films using the current-driven gated van der Pauw method.
Conventional transfer and output characteristics were employed as
contact-sensitive reference measurements and to verify the field-effect
operation of the fabricated structures. The central analysis was based on
four-terminal sheet-conductance measurements, complemented by an assessment of
the complete current-injection path and by transient and circuit measurements.

The fabricated gated thin-film structures show clear $p$-type field-effect
behaviour, low gate leakage, and reproducible transfer and output
characteristics. Their gradual turn-on and finite background resistance
nevertheless demonstrate that two-terminal device measurements include contact
and series contributions. In the gated van der Pauw measurements, the
conductance curves obtained at imposed currents of $-100\,\mathrm{nA}$,
$-200\,\mathrm{nA}$, and
$-300\,\mathrm{nA}$ collapse onto a common approximately linear dependence on
effective gate bias. The resulting contact-independent thin-film mobility is
approximately $3.1\,\mathrm{cm^2\,V^{-1}\,s^{-1}}$, with a threshold voltage
near $-10.2\,\mathrm{V}$. Analysis of the complete current-injection path gives
a residual resistance between $5.0\,\mathrm{M\Omega}$ and
$5.8\,\mathrm{M\Omega}$, consistent with the background contribution inferred
from the conventional linear-regime measurements.

Transient and amplifier measurements further show that the electrical response
is not purely static. Slow relaxation, parasitic capacitances, and large circuit
resistances can influence the measured response. Overall, the results
demonstrate that the gated van der Pauw method provides a robust description of
long-range transport in the gate-induced accumulation sheet of an organic thin
film. Here, contact independence means that voltage drops at the
current-injecting contacts do not directly enter the four-terminal transverse
voltage. Stable charge injection, low leakage, linear-response operation, film
uniformity, and temporal stability remain necessary for reliable extraction.
